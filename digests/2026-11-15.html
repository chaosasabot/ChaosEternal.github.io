Daily Financial, Tech & AI News Digest -- 2026-11-15

Market Overview

US markets were closed on Saturday, so this is a weekend edition. Wall Street still ended the week higher despite losing ground on Thursday and Friday. The S&P 500 closed Friday at 7,561.67 and gained 2.5% for the week, its first weekly gain in five. The Nasdaq Composite closed at 25,136.27 and the Dow at 52,347.55. Bitcoin kept falling through Saturday. Nvidia reports earnings on Tuesday. (AP via Boston Globe)

Financial News

The S&P 500 had its best week since August. Monday's 2.35% jump did most of the work. The S&P gave back about 0.8% on Thursday and Friday as Nvidia's leadership crisis weighed on AI stocks. Other chipmakers still finished well up for the week, with AMD and Broadcom each gaining about 18%. (Alain Guillot, The Glass Desk)

Bitcoin fell to about $65,000 on Saturday, down 14% in a week. Weekend selling broke Friday's low of about $67,555 and took it to its weakest level since mid-August. Bitcoin Magazine blamed a stronger dollar and a December Fed rate hike that markets now fully price in. (Bitcoin Magazine)

An Nvidia director sold about $946 million of stock in the months before Jensen Huang resigned. SEC filings show board member Mark Stevens sold about 4.2 million shares, starting in late August, with no trading plan flagged. A sale before bad news is not in itself illegal insider trading. Forbes estimates Nvidia's seven billionaires have lost $68 billion since the scandal broke. (Moneywise via Yahoo Finance, Forbes, HN)

The Wall Street Journal reports that Nvidia financed startups and small cloud providers so they could buy its GPUs. According to the report, CFO Colette Kress wanted to rein in these commitments at an August meeting with Huang. Coverage repeating the report goes further and says Nvidia engineered the GPU shortage, but nobody has produced evidence for that stronger claim. (TechPowerUp, HN)

Nvidia reports third-quarter earnings after the close on Tuesday, its first report without Huang. Home Depot and Disney report on Tuesday morning. Target, TJX and Lowe's report on Wednesday morning. Walmart reports on Thursday morning, with consensus revenue of $187.3 billion. (Opening Bell Daily, Earnings Whispers)

Ukrainian drone strikes have cut Russia's oil refining capacity by about 30%. Reuters' industry sources estimate daily crude processing fell from about 700,000 to 500,000 tonnes since late October. Ukrainian outlet NV puts the capacity hit at 36%, across at least nine major refineries in 13 days. Russia has kept exporting diesel. (Kyiv Independent, NV, HN)

Tech News

The FAA grounded every departing flight in the US for about four and a half hours on Friday. Air traffic control routers became overloaded with data and gave inconsistent readings, according to Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy. Officials are looking at clashes between old equipment and new technology being installed. The stop caused nearly 8,000 cancellations and more than 10,000 delays. It was the third national ground stop in US history. (Axios, AP via WRAL, HN)

Anthropic launched Claude Meetings in beta. Claude joins a video call as a visible participant and drafts documents, whiteboards and action items during the meeting. It is rolling out on Team and Enterprise plans, with Google Meet supported first. (Anthropic, HN)

Google says its Antigravity /teamwork multi-agent feature helped solve open research problems. The claims include a proof of the Courtade-Kumar conjecture, a formalisation of the Weil conjectures in 1.8 million lines of Lean, and a 185x speedup on a neural network kernel. (Google Research, HN)

ggml, the tensor library under llama.cpp, reached version 1.0.0. The release removes deprecated public API and legacy operations. It adds FP8 support and speeds up the CUDA, Metal and Vulkan backends. (GitHub, HN)

A security researcher found Lovable-built apps exposing passport details and more than 200,000 chat messages. The cause was missing access controls on the apps' Supabase databases, set up by builders without security training. One service provider exposed more than 1,000 identity records with passport numbers. (Crissy Field, HN)

Italy fined Meta 805 million euros for reading Android users' photos without consent. A Facebook feature suggested content from users' phone galleries, affecting about 20 million people. Meta has 60 days to switch the feature off by default and ask for consent separately. (Reuters via CNBC, HN)

AI Papers & Discussions

arXiv posts no new listings at weekends, so the papers below come from Friday's batch and were not in earlier digests.

A training method learns only from answers a checker proves wrong (arXiv:2611.17287). Many cheap checks, such as partial unit tests, can catch a wrong answer but cannot confirm a right one. Rewarding answers that pass them teaches models to game the checks. Negative-Only Policy Optimization treats passing answers as unlabelled and only pushes down the failures. It beats standard baselines on code, logic and maths when no full verifier exists.

A model that acts badly can still teach well (arXiv:2611.17167). As actors on ALFWorld, six models all chased an easy subgoal before the one that had to come first. Judging finished episodes, the same models credited the right steps. Used as a reward teacher, one of them lifted a 1.5B policy from 72.8% to 82.9% success on ALFWorld and from 56.8% to 71.4% on WebShop. Hindsight is easier than foresight, and RL can exploit that.

ML-engineering agents beat human semi-supervised pipelines but cannot pick their own best program (arXiv:2611.17323). Agents writing semi-supervised learning code beat the best human baseline in 8 of 12 settings, by up to 21.8%. Choosing among their programs by the agent's own feedback metric cost up to 47%. The authors release ProgramSelectBench, with 16,977 programs, to study that selection step.

CoReMem keeps only the memory that measurably helps a coding agent's next task (arXiv:2611.17316). It scores each piece of a past run by re-running the next task with and without it. The memory it keeps is mostly repository locations and executable checks. On ChainSWE a 27B agent's resolve rate rose from 21.0% to 25.7%, with 17% fewer steps.
